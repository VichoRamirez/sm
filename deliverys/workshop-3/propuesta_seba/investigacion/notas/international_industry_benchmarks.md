# International industry benchmarks for InfoCar's population, screening, segments and sample size

Research date: 2 November 2026. Scope: how leading automotive-industry studies of used-car buyers (B2C) and used-car dealers (B2B) define their population, screening, segments, sample size and field mode, and what their findings say about InfoCar's draft design (B2B: 3 dealer cells by monthly purchase volume, n=100; B2C: seller type x 4 vehicle-age bands, n=300).

Verification legend used below:
- **[V]** = verified by reading the primary document (PDF or official page) during this session.
- **[S]** = taken from a search-result snippet or a secondary/trade-press source; the primary document was not opened (paywalled, 403 error, or not found). Treat as "to confirm" before citing in the final deliverable.
- "Not disclosed" = the published material does not give the figure. No sample sizes have been estimated or invented.

## Q1. Benchmark table: how 15 industry studies define population, screening, segments, n and mode

### Takeaway
Industry studies of used-car buyers screen on a recent purchase window (4 months to 5 years; 12 months is the most common for "journey" studies). Their main split is new vs used, then seller channel (franchised dealer, independent dealer, private party). Dealer studies split franchised vs independent and weight (rather than set quotas) by sales volume. Bases per reported subgroup are usually ≥130–770 buyers, and agencies flag bases near 130–230 as "small base, interpret with caution".

### Inferences
- **Purchase-window screening.** InfoCar's "bought a used light/medium vehicle in the last 12 months" matches the Cox Car Buyer Journey window exactly. It is close to J.D. Power Japan (2–13 months of ownership), stricter than VSA BC and CCPC (5 years) and looser than CarGurus (4 months). It is the most defensible industry-standard choice.
- **Sample size per segment.** Industry used-buyer bases per reported segment run from 130 to 770 (Cox 770 used; BC 138 private / 232 dealer used). Ipsos already flags 138–232 as "small base". At n=300 across 8 cells, InfoCar has about 37 per cell. That is far below these benchmarks. The defensible way to report it is by main effect: about 150 per seller type and about 75 per age band, with cell-level results described as exploratory.
- **B2B sample size.** InfoCar's B2B n=100 is about one tenth of a single CADSI quarter (about 900–980 dealers). CADSI is a national panel of a much larger universe, so the comparison mostly shows that 100 is a student-scale, exploratory design. With 3 cells of about 33 each, cell comparisons are directional only.
- **Volume segmentation.** CADSI weights by "dealership type and sales volume". This is direct industry precedent for treating monthly volume as a dealer-stratification variable, as InfoCar does.

### Gaps
- No study found that publishes "primary/joint decision maker" or "paid with own money" as an explicit screener. Cox, CarGurus and J.D. Power publish only the purchase window. The questionnaires themselves are not public.
- Per-country Deloitte sample sizes, and whether Chile is among the 27 markets, were not found on the public pages (the full PDF, 11 MB, was not parsed).
- The CarGurus 2025 methodology could not be opened directly (HTTP 403/406). Figures come from search snippets.
- The NIADA "active dealer" threshold is behind the paid report.

## Q2. Cox Automotive: Car Buyer Journey, Dealer Sentiment Index, Manheim index and private-party coverage

### Takeaway
Cox's consumer study screens on "bought/leased in previous 12 months + used the Internet". Its dealer index samples franchised and independent dealers in roughly equal halves of about 450–500 each per quarter and weights by type and volume. Cox's market metrics (used retail SAAR) explicitly exclude private-party sales. Cox's buyer study does not publish a private-party vs dealer split.

### Inferences
- InfoCar's B2C seller-type split (private seller vs dealer/company) fills a gap the main Cox buyer study does not publish. In the U.S., private-party sales are left out of the headline retail metrics even though they are a large share of the market. Comparing private-party and dealer buyers is a defensible gap-driven design choice.

### Gaps
- No Cox study was found that reports a private-party used-buyer sample or published private-party vs dealer differences in 2023–2026.
- The Cox 2024 "45% of used sales" private-party-related estimate appeared only as a truncated snippet and was not verified.

## Q6. Vehicle-age cohorts vs price bands: which do reputable analysts use, and why

### Takeaway
Reputable analysts segment the used market by vehicle age. Common bands are 0–5 / 6–12 / 13+ (Experian), 1–3 / 4–7 / 8–12 / 13+ (DesRosiers/UCDA), ≤1 / 1–3 / … / 10–15 (Auto Trader UK) and 0–3 / 4–8 (OLX Brasil). Price bands are used to describe affordability and supply, or as weighting variables. Edmunds' data show why price bands are unstable: the same price band now holds cars 2.6–4 years older than in 2019.

### Cited Findings
- Experian aftermarket segmentation: "pre-sweet spot" 0–5 years (typically under manufacturer warranty), "sweet spot" 6–12 years (needs more advanced maintenance), "post-sweet spot" 13+ (owners put less money in because of lower value); the sweet spot held 36.1% of vehicles in operation (Q2 2023) [S] — [Experian, blog](https://www.experian.com/blogs/insights/average-vehicle-age-myth/); [Experian Q2 2023 Market Trends PDF](https://www.experian.com/content/dam/noindex/na/us/automotive/market-trends/2023-market-trends/q2-2023-experian-auto-market-trends.pdf)
- UCDA/DesRosiers (Canada, 2026): sourcing difficulty asked separately for 1–3, 4–7, 8–12 and 13+ year-old vehicles — [UCDA & DesRosiers, 2026](https://www.ucda.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/UCDA-July-2026-Results.pdf)
- Auto Trader UK: ≤1 year, 1–3 years, 10–15 years bands with diverging price trends (young cars falling, 10–15-year-olds rising) — [Auto Trader, 2023](https://plc.autotrader.co.uk/news-views/press-releases/nuanced-market-records-strong-price-growth-for-older-cars-as-younger-vehicle-values-contract/)
- OLX Brasil 2026: 0–3 and 4–8 years — [Conexão Tocantins, 2026](https://conexaoto.com.br/2026/06/03/89-dos-brasileiros-consideram-historico-veicular-fator-decisivo-na-compra-de-carros-online)
- Manheim: no age restriction, but mix-adjusted by 20 market classes and mileage-adjusted; NADA (2011) limited to ≤8 years and used model year as age [S] — [Auto Remarketing, 2011](https://www.autoremarketing.com/ar/wholesale/webb-explains-why-manheim-price-reading-differs-others)
- Price drift: the $15–20k band's average age went from 3.4 to 6 years, $10–15k from 4.7 to 8.7, $5–10k from 8.1 to 10.7 (Q2 2019 → Q2 2026) — [Autobody News, 2026](https://www.autobodynews.com/news/used-vehicle-prices-hit-q2-record-as-affordable-inventory-shrinks-edmunds-finds)
- CarGurus (2024) weights its sample by "price point" (a weighting variable, not a quota) [S] — [CarGurus, 2024](https://www.cargurus.com/about/press/consumer-insights-2024)
- Cox uses a sub-$15k price band to describe supply/affordability [S] — [Cox Automotive, 2025](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights/used-vehicle-inventory-july-2025/)
- Dealer–private price gap widens with car age (age is the theoretically relevant moderator) — [Biglaiser et al., 2020](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/intermediaries-and-product-quality-in-used-car-markets/)

### Inferences
- InfoCar's 0–5 / 6–10 / 11–15 / 16+ bands align with Experian's first break (0–5, the warranty cut-off). They also capture Auto Trader's 10–15 cohort and give a separate 16+ band that matters in an older fleet like Chile's. They are not identical to any single benchmark, so the report should state that "no universal standard exists; analysts use 3–5 age bands with a first break at 3–5 years".
- Edmunds' price-drift evidence gives a strong, citable argument for InfoCar's rationale. Price bands change meaning with inflation and supply shocks, and price is the dependent phenomenon, so quotas on price would contaminate the outcome. Recording price, luxury and brand tier as covariates, without using them as quotas, mirrors CarGurus (weighting) and Manheim (market-class mix adjustment).

### Gaps
- No analyst source explicitly argues "age instead of price because price is the outcome". This is InfoCar's own methodological reasoning, supported indirectly by the price-drift data.
- Current (2025–2026) Experian band definitions and shares were not verified on a primary page.

## Q7. Private-seller vs dealer buyers: risk perception, inspection, history reports

### Takeaway
Evidence consistently shows that private-sale buyers get fewer mechanical inspections and history reports than dealer buyers. They choose the private channel mainly for price. Peer-reviewed work finds privately sold cars are more likely to be lemons. This validates seller type as InfoCar's first B2C quota variable.

### Cited Findings
- VSA BC/Ipsos 2013: private-sale buyers received a mechanical inspection report 40% vs 51% for dealer used buyers; 55% of private buyers neither asked for nor were offered an inspection vs 38% of dealer buyers (n=138 vs n=232) — [Ipsos for VSA BC, 2013](https://vsabc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2013_IPSOS_Private-sales-survey.pdf)
- VSA BC/Ipsos 2013: vehicle history report offered without asking: 29% private vs 44% dealer; "did not ask for and was not offered": 48% private vs 24% dealer — [Ipsos for VSA BC, 2013](https://vsabc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2013_IPSOS_Private-sales-survey.pdf)
- VSA BC/Ipsos 2013: reasons to buy privately: price (net) 63%, knew the seller 24%; accident history report received 30% private vs 52% dealer; warranty information 32% vs 86% — [Ipsos for VSA BC, 2013](https://vsabc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2013_IPSOS_Private-sales-survey.pdf)
- VSA BC/Ipsos 2013: "Slightly fewer (24%) private sale buyers than dealership buyers (32%) report having any problems or issues" (dealer: 26% minor + 6% major); private buyers rated the overall experience more positively (80% top-3 box vs 67%) — [Ipsos for VSA BC, 2013](https://vsabc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2013_IPSOS_Private-sales-survey.pdf)
- CCPC 2021 (Ireland): 36% had a mechanic check the car; 24% paid for an online background check; 20% did no checks — [Ipsos MRBI for CCPC, 2021](https://assets.ccpc.ie/data/docs/default-source/about-us/rai-publications/research-reports-2021/research-report-access-to-car-history-2021.pdf)
- Consumer Reports survey (date not shown): 41% of shoppers who bought from a private party or used-car dealer had a mechanic check the car vs 36% from a new-car dealer [S, undated] — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/used-car-buying/why-haggling-for-your-next-car-really-pays)
- Emons & Sheldon (2009), Swiss inspection records: the probability that a car is a lemon increases if it was sold privately; the opposite holds for dealer-sold cars [S] — [Emons & Sheldon, 2009](https://edoc.unibas.ch/15056/); [working paper](https://boris.unibe.ch/142431/1/dp0202.pdf)
- CARFAX Spain 2026: 85% concerned about fraud; 34% scammed (self-selected sample) — [CARFAX Europe, 2026](https://www.carfax.eu/press/carfax-survey-34-percent-of-used-car-buyers-have-been-scammed-in-some-way-during-their-purchase)
- AppraisalPRO 2024: 68% trust dealers more than private sellers vs 4.6% [S, vendor] — [Auto Remarketing, 2024](https://autoremarketing.com/ar/retail/buyers-trust-dealers-over-private-sellers-but-are-wary-about-hidden-problems)

### Inferences
- Private-sale buyers combine a lower price, fewer inspections and history reports, and a higher theoretical lemon risk. That makes them the segment where "technical certainty" is scarcest and where InfoCar's B2C per-report product should have the clearest value proposition. Dealer buyers are the comparison group.
- The BC result that private buyers report fewer problems and higher satisfaction is a caution. Self-reported satisfaction may not reflect actual condition risk (selection: 24% knew the seller). InfoCar's questionnaire should separate "knew the seller" from "stranger" private sales, as Ipsos did ("Private Sale, Did Not Know Seller", n=115).

### Gaps
- The only survey with a clean private-vs-dealer split on inspection and history-report use is from 2013 (Canada). No 2023–2026 equivalent was found.
- No survey measuring **willingness to pay** for an inspection or history report, split by seller type, was found.

- InfoCar's exclusion of large franchised groups and importers is a deliberate departure from the franchised/independent industry norm. It is justified because the target is independent resellers. ### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 published size distribution of independent dealers by monthly volume (U.S., Europe or Chile) was found.
- NIADA's "active dealer" unit threshold is not public.
- The UCDA total sample size is not disclosed. The 77.2% independent / 22.8% franchised assignment is inferred from the weighted-average arithmetic, because the pie-chart labels are not legible in the text extraction.

### Inferences
- The 74% accuracy-concern figure comes from franchise dealers only. InfoCar's independent-dealer population is unmeasured on this point, which is a concrete information need InfoCar's B2B survey can fill.
- The gap between adoption (82%) and confidence (8–30%) suggests InfoCar's B2B questionnaire should measure trust and accuracy thresholds (e.g., acceptable error in repair-cost estimate) and not just intent to adopt.

### Gaps
- No independent survey of dealers' trust in AI damage detection or AI appraisal (ACV, ProovStation, UVeye, Ravin, Monk, Tractable) was found. All quantitative claims are vendor-reported.
- No survey of used-car buyers' trust in phone-photo AI inspection reports was found. The closest analogue is insurance claims (Solera).

## Q10. How InfoCar's draft design compares with the benchmarks (validation summary)

### Takeaway
Most of InfoCar's choices have direct industry or academic precedent: the 12-month purchase window, seller type as a split, age bands rather than price bands, volume-based dealer strata, and price recorded as a covariate. The main weaknesses are cell-level sample sizes (about 37 per B2C cell and about 33 per B2B cell) and the lack of a franchised/independent descriptor for comparison.

### Cited Findings
- 12-month purchase window = Cox Car Buyer Journey standard — [Cox Automotive, 2026](https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-Cox-Automotive-Car-Buyer-Journey-Study-Summary.pdf)
- Seller channel split (private vs dealer) used by Ipsos/VSA BC and CCPC/Ipsos MRBI — [Ipsos for VSA BC, 2013](https://vsabc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2013_IPSOS_Private-sales-survey.pdf); [Ipsos MRBI for CCPC, 2021](https://assets.ccpc.ie/data/docs/default-source/about-us/rai-publications/research-reports-2021/research-report-access-to-car-history-2021.pdf)
- Age bands used by Experian, DesRosiers/UCDA, Auto Trader and OLX; price bands drift in age (Edmunds) — see Q6 sources; [Autobody News, 2026](https://www.autobodynews.com/news/used-vehicle-prices-hit-q2-record-as-affordable-inventory-shrinks-edmunds-finds)
- Dealer–private gap widens with car age (seller × age interaction) — [Biglaiser et al., 2020](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/intermediaries-and-product-quality-in-used-car-markets/)
- Volume as dealer weighting variable — [Cox Automotive, 2025, CADSI](https://www.coxautoinc.com/news/q2-2025-cadsi)
- Agencies flag bases of about 130–230 as small — [Ipsos for VSA BC, 2013](https://vsabc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2013_IPSOS_Private-sales-survey.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Validated:**
  - 12-month window.
  - 18+ adults (CCPC uses 18+; CarGurus uses 18–65; Deloitte uses "driving age").
  - Private vs dealer seller split.
  - Vehicle-age quotas with price recorded as a covariate.
  - Volume-based B2B strata.
  - A precise B2B respondent role.
- **Where InfoCar is stricter than the industry:**
  - The "own money" and "took part in decision" screeners. No benchmark publishes these, but they are reasonable for a willingness-to-pay objective.
  - Exclusion of franchised groups and importers.
- **Where InfoCar falls short of the benchmarks:**
  - Per-cell n (B2C about 37; B2B about 33) versus industry subgroup bases of 130–770 (B2C) and about 400–500 per dealer stratum (CADSI).
  - Recommendation: analyse main effects; treat cells as exploratory; consider fewer age bands (e.g., merge 11–15 and 16+) if fieldwork falls short.
- **Comparability add-ons:**
  - Record franchised/independent/multi-brand status.
  - Record whether a private seller was known to the buyer (Ipsos "did not know seller").
  - Record whether a mechanical inspection or history report was obtained. This allows direct comparison with the BC (40% vs 51%) and Irish (36%) inspection rates.

### Gaps
- None of the benchmarks uses InfoCar's exact 2 × 4 B2C cell structure or 3-tier monthly-volume B2B structure. The design is a justified adaptation, not a replication.
- No Chilean benchmark exists for the seller-type split or for dealer volume distribution. Cell targets cannot be set proportional to the Chilean population from published data.
