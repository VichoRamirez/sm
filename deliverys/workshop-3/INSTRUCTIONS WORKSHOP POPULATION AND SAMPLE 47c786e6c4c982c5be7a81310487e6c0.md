## DEFINING POPULATION AND SAMPLE

This project is developed cumulatively. Therefore, this submission must incorporate the corrections, adjustments, and recommendations from previous stages, keeping the entire project up to date. These improvements will be reviewed and form part of the assessment.

At this stage, the focus is an defining and characterizing the target population and sample, applying the criteria and concepts covered in class and taking into account the methodological decisions already made for the project.

The assignment must:

- Define the target population, clearly establishing who belongs to it and the characteristics used to delimit it.
- Describe the population using criteria relevant to the research problem and objectives. These may include demographic, geographic, psychographic or attitudinal, consumption-related, or needs-based descriptors; not all dimensions are mandatory. At a minimum, demographic and qualitative descriptors must be considered as the basis for defining the population. The remaining dimensions will depend on the business diagnosis and the established objectives. Justify your choices.
- Define the sample for each previously proposed methodology, specifying its size, composition, and characteristics, and justify the decisions based on the material covered in class.
- Keep all previous stages of the project updated and corrected, ensuring consistency among the diagnosis, problem, objectives, methodology/technique, population, and sample.

Note 1: Quantitative sample: Apply the principles covered in class regarding sample size and composition. As a minimum, at the minimum size segment or analysis cell, in line with the methodological content covered and available in the course materials.

Note 2: Qualitative sample: Qualitative research does not seek statistical representativeness or a large number of cases, but rather diversity and dispersion and interaction or observation settings. The sample must show exploratory, descriptive identification of relevant population characteristics or profiles. For in-depth interviews, two interviews per profiley be considered as a reference, or one per profile in an optimized sample. The same principle of diversity and dispersion applies to focus groups, ethnographies, and other proposed qualitative approaches.

Important: The focus of this submission is not to redefine research methodologies or techniques, but to appropriately develop the target population and sample based on decisions already made, incorporating corrections from previous stages.
