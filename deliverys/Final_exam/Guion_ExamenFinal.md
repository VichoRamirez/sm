## Reglas para decirlo

- **El PESTEL describe; el FODA clasifica.** Hasta la lámina 43 no se dice “opportunity” ni “threat”.
- **Cada fuerza dice de qué letra nace.** El chip amarillo (“From PESTEL: T · E”) se lee en voz alta.
- **Los límites se dicen antes de que los pregunten:** 31 % de contenido inventado y 52,3 días como proxy.
- **Las metas no son objetivos de investigación.** Un SO valida una meta; la meta es lo que hay que lograr.

## Texto

**1 · Portada**
“InfoCar. Last time we showed what we need to learn: what certainty is worth when a used car changes hands. Today we close the loop: the context around the car, the strategy that follows, and the goals that make it measurable.”

**36 · The industry**
“We define the industry as information on a used car's condition and price, before money changes hands: for a dealer, a buyer, and, as you suggested, an insurer. Five rules: industry first, built on the PESTEL, a level with reasons, high is not bad, and assumptions labelled. Where the PESTEL lacked industry data, we added it to E and T, with sources.”

**37 · Rivalry: Medium**
“The used-car market grows 3%, says The Clinic with CAVEM data. History, price and inspection are each covered by someone. Nobody joins them with the car's damage. Rivalry is medium: the contest is over who joins the layers.”

**38 · New entrants: Medium–high**
“Inchcape is piloting repair quotes from photos for BMW owners in Chile and Peru. Corfo funded 145 startups this year, and image AI costs about a dollar and a half per thousand images. Entry is cheap for the technology and costly for local data: that is where our barrier must be.”

**39 · Substitutes: High**
“The strongest force is a phone and a free chatbot: 78% of Chileans have used generative AI. But even a model anchored to car damage invents content in 31% of reports. Free is not the same as reliable. Our answer has to be proof, not promises.”

**40 · Buyers: High**
“The average dealer buys about three cars a month and can go back to a spreadsheet at no cost. Insurers are a third buyer, with 2.23 million insured cars, but Mapfre already has its own photo app. We start with a monthly plan, and SO5 asks each segment how it prefers to pay. Insurers go to the next study.”

**41 · Suppliers: Medium**
“AI vendors are many and cheap. The scarce input is workshop price data: 29 days just to get a car into a workshop, according to the CMF. That figure is for insured cars, so we use it as a proxy. Workshops are not in our sample: they are the next study.”

**42 · Five forces**
“No force is low, and two are high. As we heard in class, a high force is not a bad business: it shows what to fix before launch.”

**43–44 · Selection**
“Now we move from describing to choosing. A finding passes to the SWOT if it touches the information gap at purchase, holds today, and we can act on it now. Of 38 findings, 24 pass, 6 stay on watch, like the used-EV import bill, and 8 only frame the market.”

**45–46 · SWOT**
“Every item shows where it comes from: a PESTEL letter or a force. Opportunities: a growing market, no legal warranty on used cars, warranties that run out, insurers, and Chinese brands without a resale curve. Threats: the free chatbot, entrants within reach, early digital firms, data and AI rules, and buyers who can leave. Inside, our strength is joining damage, repair cost and price; our weaknesses are what a photo cannot see and the data we still lack.”

**47 · Four lines**
“Each line crosses inside and outside. One: proof, not promises. Two: local data as the barrier. Three: insurers as a third market. Four: subscription with an easy start.”

**48 · Goals**
“And each line has a goal. A pilot of 100 mechanic-checked reports with at most 10% invented content, against a 31% baseline. Real prices from 60 dealers, and at least three workshops sharing theirs. Three or four insurer interviews and one pilot deal. Ten dealers on the monthly plan. Research objectives tell us what to understand; these goals tell us what to achieve. Two are checked by this study, two by the next one.”

**49 · Cierre**
“Diagnosis: information asymmetry. Objective: what certainty is worth. Method: listen first, then measure. Context: PESTEL, five forces and a SWOT, every item traced. Strategy: four lines and four goals. Thank you. Questions?”
