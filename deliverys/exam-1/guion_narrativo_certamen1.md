## Slide 2 · Asimetría de información — Seba (1:15)

> Everything starts with one idea from economics: **information asymmetry**. The seller knows the car: past crashes, repaint, hidden filler, old faults. The buyer only sees an ad, a few photos and a short test drive.
>
> Akerlof called this the **market for lemons**. When buyers cannot tell a good car from a bad one, they discount every car, and the good cars leave the market.
>
> Two numbers show it in Chile. In 2025 the same model, the Ford F-150, was listed from **2.0 to 68.9 million pesos**. Price alone does not reveal condition. And when the damage finally shows up, it is slow and expensive: a car spends **52 days on average** in an insured repair workshop.
>
> So hidden condition is a cost on every deal. The question is: **who carries that cost?** Gabo.

**Concepto del curso:** el diagnóstico se apoya en un marco teórico y en datos secundarios verificables (Akerlof, 1970; Farías, 2026; Guzmán, 2026).

---

## Slide 3 · Quién carga la brecha — Gabo (1:30)

> We looked at the market from the outside in, as you asked. There were **1,060,919 used-car transfers in 2025**.
>
> **Who faces the gap?** People are **83.8%** of buyers, but a private buyer purchases a car only every few years. Our core dealers, 557 of them, buy between 3 and 27 cars a month, about 9 on average. They face the gap on every purchase. We don't drop the 84%, though: we keep both customers, and the research will compare them.
>
> **What widens the gap?** Chinese brands are already **41.4% of new-car sales** and almost 13% of used transfers. As their warranties end, they reach the used market with little local record of faults or part prices.
>
> **Who protects the buyer?** A dealer sale carries a six-month legal warranty. A private sale has no consumer-law protection, and vehicle-fraud cases rose **64% in 2025**.
>
> Three points from your feedback are on this slide: the private buyer, Chinese brands and the legal framework. So, if both sides carry the risk, **who would pay to close the gap?** Moisés.

**Concepto del curso:** muestra que se incorporó el feedback de etapas anteriores, como exige la pauta, con un análisis de situación "de afuera hacia adentro".

**Fuentes en pantalla:** CAVEM (2026a, 2026c, 2026e); ANAC (2026); Ley N.° 21.398 (2021); Melelli Viveros (2026).

## Slide 4 · Quién paga por cerrarla — Vicho (1:15)

> InfoCar builds one **condition report** from phone photos, with two ways to pay. The **dealer** pays a monthly plan and prices each car knowing the repair cost. A **person buying from a private seller**, where the law protects least, pays for a single report.
>
> Today the alternative is a mechanic: a pre-purchase inspection costs **82,000 pesos or more** and takes one to three hours. On the plan, InfoCar costs about **6,700 pesos per car** and takes minutes.
>
> To be fair, photos only see the exterior body. A mechanic also checks the engine. And these prices are **hypotheses, not conclusions**. Testing them is one of the reasons we need this research. That takes us to **what exactly we need to learn**. Joaquín.

**Concepto del curso:** distingue la hipótesis de negocio de la evidencia. El precio no se da por validado: se convierte en una necesidad de información (SO5).

## Slide 5 · Objetivos — Joaco (1:15)

> Our general objective is **to understand how technical and economic certainty is valued when setting the transaction value of used cars**.
>
> It follows the course rule: a **higher-order verb plus the phenomenon**. We chose *understand* because we are looking for meaning: how certainty becomes a price. It names **no method, no who and no where**, because those are decisions about method and sample, and they come later.
>
> We broke the general objective into five **observable dimensions**: valued attributes, cost foresight, adoption barriers, workshop experience, and appeal. Each one is **SMART** and informs one **business decision**, shown in the right-hand column.
>
> Notice the verbs. *Identify* and *describe* ask **what and why**. *Evaluate* and *analyze* ask **how much and how often**. One kind of question needs to listen, the other needs to measure. That is why our method is mixed. Moisés.

**Concepto del curso:** formulación canónica de objetivos: verbo + fenómeno; los específicos como dimensiones observables; cada objetivo informa una decisión (Scherman, 2026). El último párrafo es el puente lógico de objetivos a metodología, que es lo que evalúa la pauta.

## Slide 6 · Diseño mixto secuencial — Moisés (1:45)

> We chose a **mixed, sequential design**: qualitative first, to explore; quantitative second, to conclude. We considered three alternatives and rejected each for a reason.
>
> **Only quantitative?** No. Car appraisal in Chile has no standard. It runs on rules of thumb and informal words like *masilla* or *repintado*. A closed survey at the start would measure the wrong things.
>
> **Only qualitative?** No. Our decisions about price and segment need to know **how common** each view is and **how much** people would pay. That requires numbers we can generalize.
>
> **Mixed, but in parallel?** No, because stage 2 is **built from** stage 1. The dealers' words become our answer options, their reasons become our variables, and their objections become our scales. Running both at once would mean designing the survey blind, which is exactly the problem we are studying.
>
> Both stages cover all five objectives with different roles: stage 1 finds the words and reasons, stage 2 measures how common they are. And every answer comes from **primary data**. Secondary data supported the diagnosis, not the objectives.
>
> This choice comes from the problem, not from cost or convenience. Now, how do we listen to each customer? Daniel.

**Concepto del curso:** cubre los tres tipos de investigación de la pauta (cualitativa, cuantitativa y mixta), justifica el orden secuencial frente al paralelo y distingue información primaria de secundaria (Scherman, 2026).

## Slide 7 · Técnicas cualitativas — Dani (1:45)

> We use one technique per customer, because each one talks differently.
>
> For **dealers**, we use **one-on-one in-depth interviews** with mid-size dealer owners, appraisers and flippers. Margins and workshop deals are **trade secrets**, and nobody reveals them in front of a competitor. One-on-one, we get raw and honest answers: their hidden pricing rules, how far they trust AI, and what fails in tools like GoAuto or Rodar. The instrument is a semi-structured guide of 30 to 35 minutes.
>
> For **private buyers**, we use **moderated focus groups** with people who bought a used car in the last 12 months or plan to buy one. Fear of fraud is **social**. When one person tells how they were cheated, others open up. The group gives us shared perceptions, the buyer's own words and a first reaction to the report. The instrument is a discussion guide for 6 to 8 people, 75 to 90 minutes.
>
> We also considered **observation**. It shows what dealers do, but not why they price the way they do, and that "why" is what our objectives ask for. With these words in hand, we can measure. Bruno.

*[Si la foto del slide es de una visita a terreno, mencionarlo en una frase: es evidencia de fuente primaria.]*

**Concepto del curso:** justifica cada técnica por el problema (secreto comercial → entrevista; fenómeno social → grupo) y descarta explícitamente la observación, como pide la pauta ("why it is superior").

## Slide 8 · Técnicas cuantitativas — Bruno (1:30)

> For **dealers**, we go **face to face, in situ**, to their workplace in car hubs such as Gran Avenida and Avenida Bilbao. In situ means a setting directly tied to the phenomenon: the place where they buy and price cars. Dealers rarely answer web surveys, so going to them is the only reliable way to reach them. We use a structured questionnaire on a **tablet** to show the report prototype, kept to **8 to 10 minutes** because they are working. **CATI**, by phone, covers dealers in regions.
>
> For **private buyers**, we use **CAWI**, an online survey with **quotas** by gender, age, car age and buying channel. Buyers are many and spread out, and an online survey reaches them at scale. If a quota fills slowly, we add a **booster**: intercepts at **high-traffic locations** linked to the purchase, such as notaries and vehicle inspection plants.
>
> Both surveys show the **same report mock-up**, so we can compare dealers and buyers on the same stimulus. Each mode reaches one customer; together they cover both sides of the deal. How does this connect to our objectives? Joaquín.

**Concepto del curso:** usa los modos de administración de la pauta (F2F en el lugar de trabajo / in situ, alto tráfico, CATI, CAWI y su combinación) y explica cómo se complementan.

## Slide 9 · Matriz método → objetivos — Joaco (1:30)

> This matrix closes the loop. Read each row from left to right: **explore, measure, decide**.
>
> Take **SO2**, cost foresight. In the interviews we learn how dealers discount a car when they don't know the repair cost. In the survey we test it with a **choice experiment**: the same car, priced blind or with the repair cost known. The output is a business decision: the offer rule and the margin.
>
> Or **SO5**, appeal. The focus groups give us the first reaction to the prototype. The surveys measure intent with a concept test and price sensitivity with **Van Westendorp**. The output is the B2B and B2C prices that, two slides ago, were only hypotheses.
>
> Every objective has a technique, and every technique has a purpose. As the brief says, population and sample size come in the next stage. There we will define the segments explicitly, for example price bands, car age or brand tier. Daniel will close.

**Concepto del curso:** operacionaliza los objetivos (dimensión → pregunta → medida → decisión) y anticipa la próxima etapa con los criterios de segmentación vistos en la clase de definición de muestra.
